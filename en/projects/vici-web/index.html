  1. Task

After a rebrand, build a new website: minimalist, easy to use on any device, and one that makes people stay longer and then buy the products in a store. The site has no cart.

  1. Research

Why people visit. Not for the specs of herring, but for an answer to “what should I cook for dinner”. So we need to sell not the product but a delicious dish.

Where shoppers got lost. The catalog and recipes weren’t connected: people opened the catalog, hit a dead end and left. So the sections need to be linked.

Where they visit from. Often from a phone, right at the shelf. So the site must be easy to use with one hand.

  1. Hypotheses

A — just a beautiful catalog. Modern, but the dead ends remain.

B — a separate recipe blog. Inspiring, but doesn’t lead to a purchase.

C — a cooking magazine where recipes and products lead to each other.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I chose hypothesis C: it’s the only one that both keeps people on the site and sends them to the store with a ready shopping list.

  1. Designing the mockups

  1. Products and recipes are linked. A product has “What to cook with it”, a recipe has “Ingredients from VICI”. Why: people move from inspiration to purchase and back, and the site ranks better in search.

  1. Similar recipes. Similar dishes below each recipe. Why: there’s always a next step, so people don’t leave.

  1. Tags like “Quick”, “For breakfast”, “Seafood”. Why: the right recipe is a couple of taps away.

  1. A mobile version for the shopper at the shelf. Large buttons, the menu always at hand, recipes easy to scroll with one hand. Why: the site helps right at the moment of choice.

  1. Presentation and defense

The client’s main question in projects like this is how to tell that a site without a cart sells. The answer: we track how many people go from recipes to products, how many pages they view and how long they stay. And recipes are at the center because people come for a dinner idea, and that’s what brings them to the shelf.

  1. Handoff to development

I handed developers a clean file with a design system and a ready map of which recipes lead to which products — so the site was built quickly.

  1. Results

More pages per visit: people move from products to recipes and back.

More purchases in stores: people leave the site with a dinner plan and know which pack to look for.

The site became a “cookbook in your pocket” that people want to come back to.

The brand got younger: modern design and beautiful food attracted a younger audience.

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Analysis. AI went through customer reviews, popular queries and the old site structure in hours instead of days.

  • Content links. It quickly matched recipes with the brand’s products — I showed the client a prototype with real content.

  • Tags. It tagged the whole recipe database.

  • Mobile grids and routine. AI built the basic grids and prepared the file, and I spent the time saved on details that affect usability.
