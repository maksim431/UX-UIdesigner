  1. Project at a glance

  • Role. The only designer on the team.

  • Timeline. 4 months.

  • Starting point. A redesign after a rebrand: the old site did not help sell the products.

  • Outcome. A new site for all devices where recipes and products are linked to each other.

  1. The task

Make a new site in the refreshed brand style where people spend more time and then buy the products in a store. The site has no cart — it sells through ideas.

The client’s technical condition: pages must open quickly over mobile internet right in the store, so no heavy video or animation.

  1. What I found out

Why people come. Not for fish specs but for an idea of what to cook. So the site is a cooking magazine.

Where the dead ends are. On the old site products and recipes lived separately — people hit a dead end and left.

Where they visit from. Often from a phone right at the shelf. So everything must be easy one-handed.

  1. Three hypotheses and how I chose

I compared the hypotheses with four questions: will people stay longer, will they get to a purchase in the store, is it convenient on a phone, and what is the implementation cost.

A — a beautiful catalog. More modern, but the dead ends remain.

B — a separate recipe blog. Inspiring, but it does not lead to a product.

C — a magazine where recipes and products are linked. Inspiration turns straight into a shopping plan.

I chose C — only it both keeps people on the site and leads them to the shelf.

  1. Where I was wrong and what I changed

A recipe that led nowhere. In the first version I kept the old structure: products separate, recipes separate. When I walked the shopper path “looking for a dinner idea → figuring out what to buy” through the prototype, it broke off at the end of a recipe. That is how the links between recipes and products appeared — the project’s key decision.

A blue that did not read. The brand blue on a white background was too pale for text — a contrast check showed this. I adjusted the shade for text and kept the color’s character in large accents.

  1. What I did and why

  1. Products and recipes are linked. The product card has “What to cook with it”, the recipe has “Ingredients from VICI”.

    Why: people leave with a dinner plan and know which pack to look for.

  1. Similar recipes. After a recipe the site suggests the next one.

    Why: there is always somewhere to go next.

  1. “Quick”, “For breakfast”, “Seafood” tags. In recipes and products.

    Why: the right thing is found right away.

  1. A mobile version for the shopper at the shelf. Large buttons and a recipe that is easy to scroll one-handed. So pages open fast in the store, the beauty comes from photos and type, not video.

    Why: the site helps right at the shelf, like a cookbook in your pocket.

  1. Defending the design and the developer handoff

The client’s main question was how to tell whether a site without a cart helps sales. The answer: we look at how many people go from recipes to products, how many pages they view and whether they come back from their phone.

I gave the developers tidy layouts and a ready map of which recipe leads to which product.

How I work. When the prototype showed a dead end in my own first version, I reworked the structure instead of defending the convenient solution.

  1. Results

I do not have post-launch numbers yet, so I describe what I changed and what it should affect.

What became easier on the site

  • No dead ends: from a recipe to a product and back. More people will find the right pack.

  • The next recipe is always close — people stay longer.

  • Tags — the right thing is found faster.

  • A light mobile version — quick and convenient right in the store.

How the site tells the brand’s story

  • It sells a dish, not a product — like a cooking magazine.

  • The brand’s products are visible in every recipe.

  • A light, modern style helps move away from the “cheap frozen food” image.

  1. How AI helped

  • Claude — collecting and analyzing data. Reviews, search queries and recipe-to-product links. Far more sources, so the findings are more accurate. 5 minutes instead of a whole day of manual search.

  • Claude + Figma Agent — preparing the file for developers. Styles, layer names, versions for different screens, the design system, checks for missing screens and user-flow logic. 1 hour instead of 16 hours of work.

AI saves time; the decisions are mine.
