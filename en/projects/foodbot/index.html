FoodBot

FoodBot is a SaaS platform that lets restaurants launch their own WebApp chatbots inside Telegram to take food delivery orders — without writing any code.

in this case

  1. A café owner doesn’t sit at a computer. So the admin panel moved into their pocket.

    Bot management in a Telegram Mini App — 40% fewer missed orders.

  2. Two words that were driving clients away: “API token”.

    Five clear steps instead of one scary field — 25% less onboarding drop-off.

  3. Support questions that were never asked.

    Contextual hints: −20% support requests and bots launched 30% faster — with no new features.

  4. ~324 screens. 3 platforms. One design system.

    And new sections are built 20% faster.

  1. Task

Design an intuitive, modern UX/UI for an admin panel (personal account) for restaurant owners. The interface must be clear to users without technical knowledge. Setting up a complex bot should be as easy as filling in a social media profile.

  1. Solution

Product research

  1. The target audience is owners of small and medium HoReCa businesses (cafés, burger joints, sushi bars, coffee shops). They have very little free time and are always on the move. They often process orders slowly or miss them altogether because there are no notifications.

  2. Existing services offer flexible bot setup with a huge range of features. I studied competitors and saw that they don’t help users complete the target action at all. Users have to spend extra time figuring things out by trial and error. As a result, the first setup and bot launch take longer, which is critical for business owners.

To speed up UX research, I exported workflows, documentation and user reviews of competitor platforms and analyzed this data with an LLM. AI helped me quickly cluster the key pain points and reveal a systemic weakness of the market. This saved hours of manual audit and let me focus right away on solutions that lower the entry barrier.

Hypothesis

Restaurant owners are scared by terms like “API token” and complex multi-level bot settings. If we break the technical integration into simple step-by-step instructions, turn menu management into visual cards and add contextual hints, we will reduce cognitive load, shorten the time to complete the target action and reduce the number of support requests. If we build an adaptive version for Telegram Mini App and MAX, we will reduce order processing time and the number of missed orders, because all the functionality will be available on mobile with notifications.

What I did

  1. Simplified the technical steps: On the bot connection screen, instead of a single intimidating input field, I designed a linear, numbered guide of 5 simple steps for working with BotFather. The text is written in plain language, with key commands highlighted and links.

I fed the dry technical BotFather documentation to a neural network with a prompt to adapt the steps for a non-technical audience and break them into short instructions. This saved time on manually editing interface copy.

  1. Designed different screen states depending on where the user is in the product. Take the main bots screen as an example. When no bots have been created yet, a single centered CTA minimizes cognitive load and firmly guides the user to the main action — activation (creating the first bot).

    Once a bot is created, the most frequent task becomes managing bots, so the attention pattern naturally shifts to the bot cards and the filter/search bar. Meanwhile, the create-bot button stays in a warm attention zone.

I vibe-coded a tool that analyzes interfaces and builds a heatmap based on a specific target audience. The tool confirmed that attention is distributed exactly as intended. This let me mathematically justify the interface decisions and save resources on classic A/B testing.

  1. Contextual help. I introduced a system of contextual hints into the interface. Next to non-obvious settings there are help triggers that open modals with clear explanations, sample data and a preview of the result. The user instantly understands how a feature works or what to enter. All of this happens without leaving the current screen or contacting support, which lowers the entry barrier and saves the user’s precious time.

  1. Designed a Telegram Mini App version of the admin panel. This was a critical business decision. Restaurant owners and managers rarely sit at a desktop — they are always on the floor or on the road. Moving the control panel into a Telegram mini app let them put dishes on the stop list, change prices, send newsletters and manage orders on the go from their smartphone, and always stay on top of new ones.

The draft screens for the Telegram Mini App were assembled automatically by an AI agent in Figma based on Telegram’s native UI kit. This removed the mechanical routine of placing basic components and sped up the design stage several times over.

  1. Developer-friendly handoff: The design project is built systematically. All screens, element states and modals are carefully organized, built with Auto Layout and linked to a global UI kit with a design system, styles and components in all their states. Animations are annotated. The mockups are handed off clean and predictable, which makes implementation easy for everyone on the team and cuts development time several times over.

Renaming layers, creating styles and variables, removing unnecessary Auto Layouts, structuring the component tree, auto-generating text comments and building a convenient design system for developers and other team members — all of this was done with a Figma agent. It saved me hours on organizing the file.

  1. Result


  • Lower entry barrier and 25% less drop-off: The interface no longer looks like a “complex database” and feels like a familiar builder or social network.


  • Setup time cut by 30%: Thanks to step-by-step guides and contextual hints, users can assemble a menu and launch a bot faster and without mistakes.


  • Support load down 20%: Contextual hints answer most user questions right in the interface before people get confused.


  • 40% fewer missed orders: With the mobile version in Telegram Mini App, business owners got a full delivery control panel right in their pocket, which dramatically sped up their reaction to what’s happening in the restaurant.


  • Development 20% faster thanks to scalability: Components and styles laid the foundation for quickly adding new platform modules without breaking the overall visual style.
