FoodBot

FoodBot is a SaaS platform that lets restaurants launch their own WebApp chatbots in Telegram to take food delivery orders — without writing any code.

in this case

  1. Orders were lost not because of the menu, but because café owners don’t sit at computers.

    The admin panel moved into a Telegram Mini App — 40% fewer missed orders.

  2. Two words cost the service a quarter of new clients: “API token”.

    A step-by-step guide instead of one field — −25% onboarding drop-off.

  3. “More features than competitors” — the hypothesis we dropped.

    Why an interface that guides by itself won.

    How a heatmap for the target audience confirmed the decisions without an expensive test.

  1. Task

Design a clear, modern UX/UI for an admin panel (personal account) for restaurant owners.

The key requirement: the interface must be understandable to people without technical knowledge — setting up a complex bot should be as easy as filling in a social media profile.

  1. Research

The target audience. Small and medium HoReCa business owners: cafés, burger joints, sushi bars, coffee shops. They have little free time, are always on the move and often process orders slowly or miss them altogether because they don’t see notifications. Takeaway: management has to be at hand — on the phone, with notifications.

Competitors. Services offer flexible setup and lots of features but don’t help users complete the target action. People figure things out by trial and error, and the first setup drags on — critical for a business owner. Takeaway: the interface has to guide people by the hand.

Interface language. Restaurant owners are scared by terms like “API token” and multi-level settings. Takeaway: technical steps need to be translated into plain language.

  1. Hypotheses

Hypothesis A — more features than competitors. Win on capabilities. But new settings only raise the entry barrier that already scares people away.

Hypothesis B — training and support. Video tutorials and fast support will help people figure things out. But it shifts the complexity to support and doesn’t solve missed orders.

Hypothesis C — an interface that guides by itself. A step-by-step guide instead of technical fields, the menu as visual cards, contextual hints and an adaptive version for Telegram Mini App and MAX — to manage delivery from the phone and get notifications.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I went with hypothesis C: it reduces cognitive load and the time to launch a bot, cuts support requests and moves management to where the owner spends the whole day — the phone. Hypothesis A would amplify the main problem, and hypothesis B would treat the symptom rather than the cause.

  1. Designing the mockups

  1. Connecting a bot in 5 steps. Instead of one intimidating input field — a linear, numbered guide to working with BotFather, written in plain language with highlighted commands and links.

    Why it works: people don’t get stuck at the very first technical step — less onboarding drop-off.

  1. Screens for each stage of using the product. While there are no bots, the center of the screen has a single button — create your first bot. Once bots exist, attention moves to their cards, filters and search, and the create button stays in a warm zone.

    Why it works: the interface leads a newcomer to activation and doesn’t get in an experienced user’s way.

  1. Contextual hints. Next to non-obvious settings there are help triggers that open a window with an explanation, sample data and a preview of the result.

    Why it works: the answer appears before people get confused — no need to leave the screen or write to support.

  1. The admin panel in a Telegram Mini App. Stop list, prices, newsletters and orders — right from the smartphone.

    Why it works: owners and managers hardly ever sit at a computer — now new orders arrive where they already spend the day.

  1. Presentation and defense

I walked the client through the whole chain: from café owners’ pain points to the decisions in the mockups.

The mobile version is a priority. The Telegram Mini App version required separate work, but it’s a critical business decision: it’s what solves the missed-orders problem.

Fewer settings in view. Instead of competing on the number of features, the interface wins because someone without technical knowledge can figure it out.

  1. Handoff to development

The design project is built systematically: screens, states and modals are built with Auto Layout and linked to a UI kit with a design system, styles and components in all states. Animations are annotated. All three platforms — landing page, admin panel and mini app — live in one design system, so new modules are added quickly without breaking the style.

  1. Results

  • Onboarding drop-off −25%: the interface no longer looks like a “complex database” and feels like a familiar builder.

  • Setup time −30%: thanks to step-by-step guides and hints, people assemble the menu and launch a bot faster and without mistakes.

  • Support requests −20%: hints answer most questions right in the interface.

  • Missed orders −40%: the delivery control panel is now in their pocket.

  • Development speed +20%: components and styles became the foundation for new modules.

3 platforms · ~174 admin panel screens (94 + 80 adaptive) · ~150+ mini app screens · 4 landing pages · 12 pages in Figma

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Data analysis. I exported workflows, documentation and reviews of competitor platforms and analyzed them with an LLM: AI clustered the pain points and revealed a systemic weakness of the market — hours of manual audit saved.

  • Interface copy. I fed the BotFather documentation to AI with the task of adapting it for a non-technical audience — the 5-step guide came together faster than with manual editing.

  • Testing without A/B. I vibe-coded a tool that analyzes an interface and builds a heatmap for a specific audience. It confirmed that attention flows exactly as intended.

  • Draft mockups. An AI agent in Figma assembled draft Telegram Mini App screens based on Telegram’s native UI kit.

  • Figma routine. Layer naming, styles and variables, Auto Layout cleanup, component structure and comments for developers — done with a Figma agent.
