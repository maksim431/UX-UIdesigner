sCPlab

SCP LAB is a mobile coaching app for professional athletes. It builds a training program, shows video lessons from pros and tracks progress.

in this case

  1. Task

The client came with a clear goal: more people should buy a paid subscription after the free trial, and starting a workout should be quick and easy. A separate wish was a premium “glass” style.

  1. Research

The path to payment. Apps usually ask for money right away or after a boring questionnaire — people leave without understanding what they’d pay for. So the value has to be shown before payment.

Where it’s used. In the gym: sweaty hands, out of breath, glare. So buttons must be large and text minimal.

What brings people back. Pros know the basics — they need video lessons from top coaches. So video is the heart of the product.

  1. Hypotheses

A — a discount on the first month. Quick, but trains people to wait for discounts, while the client needs full-price payments.

B — a shorter questionnaire, payment in the same place. Simpler, but people still pay without trying.

C — try first, pay later. A free test with video lessons, and the subscription once the personal program is ready.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I chose hypothesis C: people pay more willingly for something they’ve tried and put effort into. A discount contradicted the client’s goal, and a shorter questionnaire didn’t solve the main problem — people didn’t understand what they were paying for.

  1. Designing the mockups

  1. Payment at the end. First a free test with video lessons, then “your program is ready” and only then the subscription. Why: people pay for something that’s already theirs, not for a promise.

  1. Video is the star of the workout screen. A large player, a timer and a couple of buttons. Why: the value is always in view, and the workout starts faster.

  1. Large high-contrast buttons. Why: they’re easy to hit mid-set — the app is convenient right in the gym, so people come back.

  1. Style. A black background, a neon-yellow accent, “glass” in cards and windows. Why: it looks premium — paying full price feels fair — and it’s readable in any light.

  1. Presentation and defense

I showed the client the whole chain from research to decisions. The main thing to defend was late payment: money comes a bit later, but people pay more often and at full price. I backed the decisions with UX test results.

  1. Handoff to development

I handed developers a clean file — a ready design system with all screens and button states. Development goes faster and without extra questions, which means it costs the client less.

  1. Results

+10% subscriptions: more people buy the full subscription, because they pay for a program they’ve already tried.

30% faster from launching the app to starting a workout.

+20% returns: the app is convenient right in the gym.

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Review analysis. In a couple of minutes AI went through hundreds of competitor reviews and pulled out the main complaints. By hand it would have taken a day.

  • User journey. It helped quickly build and check the path to payment and find the best moment to offer a subscription.

  • Footage for mockups. The real videos hadn’t been shot yet, so I generated similar footage and checked in advance that text and buttons are readable over video. The client didn’t have to pay for rework after the shoot.

  • Tests and Figma routine. AI plugins showed where users look, and an AI agent prepared the file for developers many times faster.
