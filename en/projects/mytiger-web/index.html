my tiger

“My Tiger” is a premium pet clinic in Moscow: veterinary care and grooming. I built a separate landing page for it that brings in booking requests.

  1. Task

The client needed a landing page that brings in requests from ads — in addition to the main website. The wishes: brand patterns with tiger silhouettes and claw marks, real photos, a premium style and unusual solutions. And most importantly — to show that grooming here isn’t just about looks but a medical procedure done by professionals.

  1. Research

Clients choose by trust. Their pet is part of the family, so price is secondary. So the landing page has to show the people, the place and the approach quickly.

Grooming is usually sold as “wash and trim”. But the owner wants the groomer to notice a skin or ear problem and show the pet to a vet. So the “groomer + vet” combination is the main reason to choose this clinic.

Discounts look cheap in premium. “−30%” brings in bargain hunters. So special offers are needed, but without discounts.

People arrive in different ways. For a service, for a specific vet, for a special offer or for an online consultation. So every path has to lead to a short request.

  1. Hypotheses

A — a discount landing page. Quick requests, but a hit to the brand’s status and the wrong clients.

B — a short copy of the main website. Safe, but no different from the website — nothing to compare.

C — trust first, then short booking. We show vets and groomers as one team, the interiors and the approach, booking is available everywhere, and special offers are presented as care.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I chose hypothesis C: it’s the only one that accounts for clients choosing by trust, makes the “groomer + vet” combination a reason to book, keeps special offers without discounts and differs from the main website enough for a fair comparison.

  1. Designing the mockups

  1. A clear first screen. The logo, “pet beauty and health clinic”, a real photo and a booking button. Why: it’s immediately clear where you are and what to do.

  1. The “Compliment” panel always in view. On the left is a dark panel with the tiger pattern and the special offer. Why: a reason to book is always in sight, without annoying banners.

  1. Booking on top of the page. The form opens over a photo of an exam room. Why: no need to go anywhere, and the visitor already “sees” the clinic while deciding.

  1. A short form. Minimal fields, contact wherever it’s convenient: phone, Telegram or another messenger, or just a callback. Why: the less effort, the more requests.

  1. Grooming next to veterinary care. Vets and groomers are one team with experience and profiles, and the clinic’s philosophy says every haircut is also a health check. Why: the owner isn’t afraid to trust their pet and understands what they pay for.

  1. “Compliments” instead of discounts. A special price and dates without percentages, and inside — a useful article: what to watch out for and what the program includes. Why: there’s a reason to book now, and the brand doesn’t look cheaper.

  1. Online consultation. You can start with a conversation with a vet without leaving home. Why: an easy first step for those not ready to visit yet.

  1. Reviews and clinics on a map. The Yandex Maps rating, addresses and directions — at the end of the page. Why: the last doubts disappear right before the decision.

  1. Presentation and defense

On a live prototype, I showed the client the whole chain: research, hypotheses, the choice and the purpose of every decision. The main thing to defend was dropping discounts: a “Compliment” gives the same reason to book but doesn’t scare away premium clients. After the discussion we simplified booking even further — removing the choice of vet and clinic from the flow — and added online consultations.

  1. Handoff to development

I handed developers the mockups for desktop and phone, all windows and booking steps. Changes are annotated right in the mockups, so the developer doesn’t need to ask anything.

  1. Results

Metrics are being collected: the landing page is live, and we’re now comparing it with the main website by the number of requests and looking at which paths bring more clients. The results will appear here as soon as they are ready.

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Review analysis. AI quickly went through reviews of vet clinics and grooming and showed what owners are afraid of.

  • A live coded prototype. With AI I built a working prototype of the landing page. I used it to defend my work to the client and to run user tests before development.

  • Compliment texts. AI helped build a clear structure for the articles; the facts came from the clinic.

  • Figma routine. An AI agent prepared the file and components for developers.
