my tiger

“My Tiger” is a premium pet clinic in Moscow: veterinary care and grooming. I built a separate landing page for it that brings in booking requests.

in this case

  1. No discounts — and still a reason to book.

    The clinic’s “Compliments” instead of crossed-out prices.

  2. A haircut that doubles as a health check.

    Why the groomer and vet together are the main reason to book.

  3. Why we did not build a discount page.

    Three concepts and how I chose.

  4. Booking got shorter after the review.

    What we removed after walking through booking with the client.

  1. Project at a glance

  • Role. The only designer on the team.

  • Timeline. 2 months.

  • Starting point. A new page for ads — an alternative to the clinic’s main website.

  • Outcome. A landing page with easy booking and a working prototype where the client saw everything before development.

  1. The task

Make a page that brings appointment and grooming bookings from ads — in the clinic’s premium brand style with real photos. Show that grooming here is health care, not just a haircut.

The client’s technical condition: the clinic’s system does not show vets’ free time publicly, so you cannot pick an exact time on the page — an administrator handles the request.

  1. What I found out

People choose by trust. For owners a pet is family; what matters most is who will work with it and how. So we show the people and the place right away.

Grooming is sold as a haircut. Here the groomer works together with a vet. That is the key difference.

Discounts cheapen. A premium clinic needs special offers without crossed-out prices.

People arrive in different ways. For a service, a vet, a special offer or a consultation. Every path should lead to a short request.

  1. Three concepts and how I chose

I compared the concepts with three questions: does it build trust, does it keep the clinic’s status, and can it be fairly compared with the site.

A — a discount page. There will be requests, but from the wrong people.

B — a short copy of the site. Safe, but nothing to compare.

C — trust and a short path. People, interiors, booking in a couple of steps and “Compliments” instead of discounts.

I chose C. Its boldest part is dropping discounts, which I defended to the client.

  1. Where I was wrong and what I changed

Booking was longer than it needed to be. At first booking repeated the site’s logic: service → vet → clinic → form. At the review the client and I walked through it on the working prototype and saw the extra steps.

I removed the vet and clinic choice: after choosing a service, people go straight to the form. I made the online consultation a separate short path.

  1. What I did and why

  1. A clear first screen. Logo, who we are, a real photo and a “Book” button.

    Why: in a couple of seconds you know where you are and what to do.

  1. The “Compliment” panel always in view. A dark panel with the brand tiger stays put while scrolling.

    Why: a reason to book is always close, without annoying banners.

  1. Booking on top of the page. The form opens right on the landing page over a photo of the exam room.

    Why: no need to go anywhere — people do not get lost.

  1. A short form. Name, phone, pet and a preferred way to be contacted, or a callback. Since an exact time cannot be chosen, the form honestly collects contact details and the administrator agrees the time.

    Why: quick to fill in, and the reply comes where it is convenient.

  1. Grooming next to veterinary care. Vets and groomers are one team.

    Why: trusting your pet is less scary, and a haircut becomes health care.

  1. “Compliments” instead of discounts. A special price with a deadline and an explanation of why the pet needs it.

    Why: a reason to book now, without the clinic looking cheap.

  1. Online consultation. Its own short booking path.

    Why: a first step for those not yet ready to come in.

  1. Reviews and clinics on a map. Rating, addresses, opening hours and directions at the end of the page.

    Why: answers to “can I trust them?” and “how far is it?”.

  1. Defending the design and the developer handoff

I showed the client not pictures but a working page — and explained how each decision helps bookings. The main thing I defended was dropping discounts.

I gave the developers all screens, windows and booking steps, with post-review changes annotated right in the layout.

How I work. In two months I took the project from research to development on my own, and I show contentious decisions on a live prototype.

  1. Results

The page is live and numbers are being collected: we compare how many bookings come from the landing page and from the main website. As soon as there are results, they will be here.

What became easier

  • Four paths to booking: services, vets, “Compliments”, consultation.

  • Short booking right on the page.

  • Contact where it suits you: phone, messenger or a callback.

How the page explains the clinic’s value

  • Trust first — people and the place, then the request to book.

  • A haircut as health care thanks to the groomer and vet working together.

  • Special offers without discounts — the clinic stays premium.
