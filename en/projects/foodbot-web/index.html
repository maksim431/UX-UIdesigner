FoodBot

FoodBot is a service where cafés and restaurants launch their own Telegram bot for delivery orders — no programmers needed.

in this case

  1. Where does a café owner spend the day? Definitely not at a computer.

    Management moved into Telegram — 40% fewer missed orders.

  2. Two words that drove clients away.

    How “API token” turned into 5 clear steps.

  3. Support questions that were never asked.

    Hints right in the interface: 20% fewer requests.

  4. No A/B test needed.

    AI helped build a tool that shows where users look.

  1. Task

Build a dashboard that a café owner can figure out without technical knowledge. Setting up a bot should be as easy as filling in a social media profile.

  1. Research

Who the users are. Owners of cafés, burger joints, sushi bars and coffee shops. They’re always on their feet and often miss orders because they don’t see notifications. So management has to be on the phone.

What competitors offer. Lots of features, but no hints — the first launch takes hours. So the interface has to guide by the hand.

What scares people. Words like “API token”. So technical steps need to be explained in plain words.

  1. Hypotheses

A — more features than competitors. But it gets even more complicated.

B — training videos and support. Helps, but loads support and doesn’t prevent missed orders.

C — an interface that guides by itself. Simple steps, visual cards, hints and management right in Telegram.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I chose hypothesis C: it’s the only one that speeds up bot launch, cuts support questions and saves orders at the same time by moving management to the phone.

  1. Designing the mockups

  1. Connecting in 5 steps. Instead of one scary field — a clear guide. Why: people don’t abandon the service at the very first step.

  1. Screens for each situation. While there are no bots — one big “Create a bot” button, then cards and search come to the front. Why: a newcomer knows where to start, an experienced user works comfortably.

  1. Hints. Next to an unclear setting — an icon with an example. Why: no need to write to support.

  1. Management in Telegram. Stop list, prices and orders — from the phone. Why: orders arrive where the owner already spends the whole day.

  1. Presentation and defense

I showed the client the path from café owners’ pain points to the decisions. The main things to defend were the Telegram mobile version as a priority — extra work, but it’s what saves orders — and fewer settings in view: we win not on the number of features but because the service is clear without a programmer.

  1. Handoff to development

I handed developers a clean file with a design system for all three platforms. New sections are added quickly and in a consistent style.

  1. Results

  • 25% fewer people abandon the service at the start.

  • 30% faster first bot setup.

  • 20% fewer support requests.

  • 40% fewer missed orders.

  • 20% faster development of new sections.

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Competitor analysis. Within minutes AI pulled the main problems out of guides and reviews — hours of manual analysis saved.

  • Copy. It rewrote Telegram’s dry technical guide in simple words — I only had to edit it.

  • Checking without an A/B test. With AI I built a tool that shows where users look. It confirmed the decisions — an expensive test wasn’t needed.

  • Figma routine. An AI agent assembled draft Telegram screens and prepared the file for developers.
