FoodBot

  1. Project at a glance

  • Role. I was the only designer on the team and did all the design.

  • Timeline. 11 months.

  • Start. A new project from scratch. Only the site map and competitor data are on hand.

  • Bottom line: A cabinet, a Telegram widget, and a one-style landing page that work better than their competitors.

  1. The task

The task was to make a dashboard where a café owner without technical skills can launch an ordering bot on their own and not miss orders. Launching should be as easy as filling in a social media profile.

Technical condition from the client: the mobile dashboard runs inside Telegram as a mini app. Telegram has its own buttons and its own colors based on the user’s theme, and when the keyboard is open there is little room left on the screen.

  1. What I found out

Who uses it. Café owners are on their feet all day and miss orders because they don’t see notifications. So management has to be on the phone.

What competitors do. They have many features, but nobody tells you what to do next. So I decided the dashboard should guide people step by step.

Which words scare people. “API token” and dozens of settings. So the language has to be simple.

  1. Three hypotheses and how I chose

I scored the hypotheses with ICE. Impact: how much the time to bot launch and the share of missed orders would change (1 to 10). Confidence: how sure I am of the result (in percent). Ease: how easy it is to build (1 to 10). The total is the product of the three scores.

Hypothesis A. More features. The dashboard would only get more complicated.
Impact 3/10 · Confidence 30% · Ease 2/10 → ICE 1.8

Hypothesis B. Training and support. The complexity wouldn’t go away, it would just move to support.
Impact 4/10 · Confidence 50% · Ease 8/10 → ICE 16

Hypothesis C. A dashboard that guides people step by step. Step-by-step instructions, hints and management from Telegram.
Impact 9/10 · Confidence 80% · Ease 3/10 → ICE 21.6

I chose C because it has the highest ICE score: 21.6 versus 1.8 and 16. It is harder to build, but it is the only one that solves missed orders. The downside was that the mobile version in Telegram needed a lot of separate work.

  1. Where I was wrong and what I changed

A blue that was hard to read. The main blue on a white background turned out too pale, and captions and links were hard to read, especially on a phone. I noticed this when I checked the mockups for contrast. I adjusted the shade, and now everything reads well while the color stays recognizable.

Testing without an expensive test. With the help of AI I built a tool that shows where users look. It showed that attention follows the path I planned.

  1. What I did and why

  1. Connecting in 5 steps. Simple step-by-step instructions instead of one technical field.

    Why: so people don’t get stuck on the very first step.

  1. Screens for each situation. While there are no bots, the screen has one “create” button. Once bots exist, you see a list, filters and search.

    Why: a beginner knows where to start, and an experienced user can manage things easily.

  1. Hints. Next to complex settings there is an explanation with an example.

    Why: the answer is already on the screen, so there is no need to write to support.

  1. Management in Telegram. Orders, prices and the stop list can be changed from a smartphone. To fit Telegram’s conditions, I built the screens from its native elements: familiar buttons, colors based on the user’s theme and short forms above the keyboard.

    Why: orders arrive where the owner already spends the day.

  1. Defending the design and the developer handoff

I showed the client everything in order: from café owners’ problems to the decisions. We discussed the mobile version in Telegram the most. It is expensive, but it is exactly what keeps orders from being lost.

I handed the developers a tidy file. The dashboard, mini app and landing page are made in one design system, so new sections are added quickly.

How I work. For eleven months I was the only designer, so I explained every decision through the business goal.

  1. Results

What became easier in the product

  • Connecting in 5 steps: 25% fewer drop-offs.

  • Hints: setup is 30% faster, and there are 20% fewer support requests.

  • Management in Telegram: 40% fewer missed orders.

  • One design system: new sections are developed 20% faster.

How the product explains itself

  • Simple language instead of technical terms.

  • Looks like a familiar builder, not a complex database.

  • The menu as cards, the same way a guest will see it.
