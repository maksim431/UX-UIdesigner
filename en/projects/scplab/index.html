sCPlab

SCP LAB is a mobile app that acts as a personal trainer for professional athletes. It analyzes the user’s physical data and generates an adaptive training plan to build strength. The core features are video instructions from professionals with individually tuned load, a progress tracker, smart reminders and weekly performance analytics.

  1. Task

Design the UX/UI of a mobile app. The goal is to cut the time it takes to start a workout and to increase conversion from the trial to a paid full-price subscription. The client asked for a premium glass effect.

  1. Solution

Research

Competitor audit. Why? To take the best, avoid their mistakes and add features and design touches that make us stand out.

Conversion funnel analysis. The standard user journey in fitness apps. The paywall (payment screen) usually appears either right at launch or after a dull text questionnaire (height, weight, goal). For an athlete to buy a full-price subscription, they need to feel the “endowment effect” before paying.

Context of use. The conditions in which a PRO athlete uses their phone during a heavy workout. In the gym the user has sweaty hands, shakes after a set, is out of breath, and the lighting can cause strong glare. There is no time to read small text.

The role of content in retention. What will bring a professional back to the app (they already know the basic technique)? The product’s core value is video instructions from top professionals with unique technique.

Using AI, I scraped large volumes of user reviews of competitor apps from the App Store and Google Play and ran them through a neural network for semantic analysis and clustering of UX pain points. As a result, AI helped me extract the key patterns in a couple of minutes.

Hypothesis

Professional athletes can’t be won over by dry promises on a paywall screen. If we move the paywall and first engage the user in free interactive testing with high-quality video instructions, letting them invest physical effort into the app, the value of a personal program becomes tangible (the IKEA effect). Large video formats, high-contrast indicators and lower cognitive load while studying quality video lessons all justify the price of the subscription.

What I did

  1. Moved the paywall (UX funnel): I rebuilt the onboarding architecture. The user doesn’t hit a hard payment screen at the start. First they go through an initial physical test with a metronome, guided by professional video lessons. Payment is requested only after the trial period ends, when the system has analyzed the data and reports that the personal program is ready.

I used an LLM to analyze UX patterns in the fitness segment and to model the user journey (CJM). AI helped me quickly design the funnel logic, structure the onboarding screens and mathematically justify the best moment to show the paywall.

  1. Focus on video content (UI): Since the product’s main value is the expertise of professional coaches, the active workout screens are built around a large video player. It takes up most of the screen, removing visual noise and leaving only the exercise itself, a timer and basic controls.

While assembling the mockups, I used neural networks to generate realistic stock footage of workouts with complex lighting and motion. This let me test how legible the UI is on top of video and calibrate the interface contrast before the client spent money on real video production.

  1. Less friction in the interface (UX): During a workout it’s hard for an athlete to hit small elements on the screen. So I designed the UI elements to be large and high-contrast.

I used AI plugins in Figma for testing (heatmaps) and simulating visual perception. As a result, I calculated ideal tap-target proportions and saved hours of manual contrast testing.

  1. Adapted glassmorphism and liquid glass (UI): I used a deep black background with an aggressive neon-yellow accent. This is the best combination for legibility under any lighting in the gym. I applied the glass effect functionally: translucent blurred backgrounds are used in cards, modals and alerts. This gave the interface premium depth without distracting from the video.

  1. Developer-friendly handoff: The design project is built systematically. All screens, element states and modals are carefully organized, built with Auto Layout and linked to a global UI kit with a design system, styles and components in all their states. Animations are annotated. The mockups are handed off clean and predictable, which makes implementation easy for everyone on the team and cuts development time several times over.

With an AI agent in Figma I automated organizing the file (bulk layer naming, creating styles, a design system with descriptions) and checking the UI kit for consistency and all the required component states. This let me prepare a perfectly clean file for developers and designers many times faster.

  1. Result

Full-price conversion up 10%: Moving payment to the end of the trial sharply increased the motivation to buy. The athlete pays for a specific program already calculated for their physiology, one they invested their own effort into during the trial.

Time-to-value down 30%: Thanks to video-first layouts and minimalist navigation, users spend minimal time setting up the app before a set and go straight to the target action.

Retention up 20%: Attractive design combined with barrier-free UX (easy control with sweaty hands, high-contrast alerts) built a habit of using the service not just as a tracker but as a full-fledged premium coach at every workout.
