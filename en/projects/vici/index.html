VICI is a large food company and one of Europe’s leading producers of fish and seafood.

The brand is best known for its crab sticks (made from surimi fish paste) — its signature product. For decades the red packs with the VICI logo were the most popular product in this category across the post-Soviet countries.

in this case

+35% page depth and +40% time on site — a frozen fish catalog became a cooking magazine.

+25% clicks from recipes to products — a website without a cart that still sells.

−20% mobile bounce rate — the site is designed for a shopper standing at the shelf.

+12 pp of 18–34 audience — a “cheap frozen food” brand got younger without changing the product.

  1. Task

Design a minimalist website based on the brand’s new design code (after a rebrand). The main business goal: design the interface to increase the time users spend on the site and build deferred demand (the desire to buy the products in an offline store). Adapt the site to all devices without losing its visual appeal.

  1. Solution

Research

I ran a product research stage to find where the business goals (offline sales growth) meet real shopper behavior.


  1. Consumption patterns

    Why would an ordinary person visit the website of a frozen seafood producer at all? People rarely look for the specs of herring or shrimp. Their real need is to answer “what should I cook for dinner” or to find an idea for a festive table. We need to sell not the raw product but the end result — a delicious dish. The site should work not as a warehouse showcase but as a lifestyle cooking magazine.


  2. Site map

    How the main sections (Catalog, Recipes, About the brand) were connected. The “Products” and “Recipes” sections existed in isolation. A user would open the fish catalog, hit a dead end and leave (a high bounce rate). It became clear that cyclic cross-linking was needed: the catalog should suggest recipes, and a recipe page should lead back to specific brand SKUs to add to a shopping list.

I used an LLM to process large volumes of unstructured data (customer reviews, frequent search queries) and to run a quick audit of the old site architecture. This saved hours of manual research and gave me a solid, data-based foundation to move quickly to a product hypothesis.

Hypothesis

If we move away from the “dry manufacturer catalog” format and build the user journey around emotional content (recipes) with seamless UX links between inspiration and a specific product, users will spend more time on the site. This will give them ready-made consumption scenarios, which turn into buying the product from a supermarket shelf.

What I did

  1. I built a two-way link between the catalog and recipe sections. On every product page (for example, crab sticks or shrimp) a “What to cook with it” block shows relevant recipes. And vice versa: a specific recipe page (for example, a trout bagel) has an “Ingredients from VICI” block linking straight to the product page. This pulls the user into a browsing loop (an “engagement loop”) and also helps the site rank in search engines.

  1. On the recipe page I introduced smart recommendations in the footer (sticky, or infinite scroll on mobile). After reading a recipe for shrimp croissants, the system suggests other quick breakfasts. This is a critical decision for increasing Time on Site.

I used AI to analyze the recipe database and product catalog and quickly generate a cross-link matrix. AI helped map ingredients to specific brand SKUs, saving hours of routine data grouping while designing the information architecture. As a result, I was able to build a prototype with realistic content faster, making it clearer for the client.

  1. In the recipes and products sections I introduced a convenient tag system (for example, “Quick”, “For breakfast”, “Seafood”). It lets users find the content they need instantly, without long searches or page reloads, which lowers the bounce rate.

I used an LLM to cluster the content base (quickly tagging recipes by consumption scenario, time and ingredients) so I could hand developers a ready content-link schema and speed up development.

  1. The mobile version was designed with the idea that the user might be standing right at a supermarket shelf. I enlarged tap targets, made the navigation sticky (sticky header) and laid out the recipes so the step-by-step instructions are easy to scroll with one hand without losing focus on the brand’s ingredients.

The basic mobile grids were generated with a neural network. Delegating this routine re-layout to AI saved hours of mechanical work. I spent the freed-up time on careful, hands-on refinement of the critical UX scenarios.

  1. The design project is built systematically. All screens, element states and modals are carefully organized, built with Auto Layout and linked to a global UI kit with a design system, styles and components in all their states. Animations are annotated. The mockups are handed off clean and predictable, which makes implementation easy for everyone on the team and cuts development time several times over.

I delegated routine technical work to AI. AI-based tools were used to automatically structure and rename layers, quickly validate missing styles and generate the design system. After my review, this let me hand the project to the development team in record time.

  1. Result

Page depth +35%: Thanks to two-way cross-linking (Product ↔ Recipe), users stopped ending the session after one page and moved along the “engagement loop”. Average time on site grew by 40%.

Hidden conversion (sales) +25%: 25% more clicks from recipes to product pages. Users leave the site not just with an abstract idea of the VICI brand but with a concrete dinner plan (for example, a hearty potato salad with herring) and an understanding of which pack to look for on the store shelf.

A convenient mobile experience: −20% bounces, +15% return visits: Adapting to mobile patterns turned the site into a handy “cookbook in your pocket” that people want to come back to, which increased user LTV (lifetime value) in their relationship with the brand.

A younger audience, +12 pp: The share of visitors aged 18–34 grew by 12 percentage points. A modern, airy UI and a focus on food aesthetics helped the brand shed its “cheap frozen food” image and attract a younger audience that values visuals and quick, tasty recipes.
