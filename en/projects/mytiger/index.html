my tiger

“My Tiger” is a pet beauty and health clinic in Moscow: veterinary care and grooming under one roof. I designed a separate landing page for the clinic to collect booking requests — an alternative to the main website that can be run in ads and compared with it on conversion.

in this case

Grooming as a medical procedure — how to show that a haircut starts with caring for health, not with scissors.

Promotions without discounts — how to fit special offers into a premium brand without lowering its status.

Booking without extra forks — from anywhere on the page straight to a short form.

Metrics are being collected — the landing page is live, results will appear here.

  1. Task

Design a landing page that converts visitors into booking requests for appointments and grooming — as an alternative to the clinic’s main website.

The client’s constraints: brand patterns (tiger silhouettes and claw scratches) and real photos, a premium style and unconventional design solutions. A separate goal was to show the clinic’s approach: grooming here is not a cosmetic service but a full medical procedure that requires a professional approach.

  1. Solution

Research

I looked at who comes to a premium clinic and what stops them from leaving a request.

The audience. Dog and cat owners for whom the pet is part of the family. They choose a clinic not by price but by trust: who will treat and groom their pet, in what conditions, and how animals are treated here.

How grooming is sold. For most people grooming means “wash and trim”, and salons compete on price. A premium owner cares about something else: that the groomer notices a problem with the skin, ears or coat and passes the pet to a vet in time.

Promotions and premium. Discounts, crossed-out prices and “−30%” are the usual way to attract customers, but in the premium segment they read as a sign of cheapness. Premium brands don’t run discount sales — yet the clinic still needs special offers.

Entry points. One owner comes for a specific service, another for a vet they trust, a third for a special offer. Each of these paths has to lead to a request quickly.

Hypothesis

If we show the clinic through its people, interiors and approach — vets and groomers working as one team, grooming next to veterinary care — and make booking short from anywhere on the page, owners will leave requests more often. Special offers presented as care and an expert program rather than a discount will attract clients without lowering the brand’s status.

What I did

  1. A premium style with brand patterns (UI): Deep green, warm milky white and chocolate — a palette that echoes the clinic’s interiors. The brand patterns — tiger silhouettes and claw scratches — live on the dark side panel and in the details: the logo, dividers and accents. A large logo on the first screen and real photos of pets and specialists instead of stock images set a premium tone.

  1. An unconventional layout (UI/UX): On the left is a pinned dark panel with the brand pattern and the clinic’s “Compliment”; it stays on screen while scrolling, so the special offer is always at hand. On the right is the content: a first screen with the logo and a booking button, the clinic’s philosophy, online consultations, services, the interior, vets and groomers, reviews and clinics on a map. Booking opens on top of the page over a photo of an exam room — the visitor keeps the context and feels like they are already in the clinic.

  1. Grooming as a medical procedure (UX/content): Veterinary services and grooming stand side by side as equals — in the “Services” block and in a shared “Vets and groomers” team. Every specialist has their experience and a detailed profile. “Our philosophy” says directly that the groomers are trained in the specifics of each breed and work together with the vets, so every spa treatment or haircut is also a check of the pet’s health.

  1. Promotions without discounts — “Compliments” (UX/content): Instead of promotions there are the clinic’s “Compliments”: special prices and programs with validity dates, with no percentages or crossed-out prices. Each compliment opens as an expert article. For example, the gastro check-up starts with the symptoms worth paying attention to, then shows what the program includes and its special price. The owner gets care and expertise rather than a discount, and the brand’s status doesn’t suffer.

  1. Short booking from anywhere (UX): You can book through a service, through a specialist, via a compliment, or just leave a number for a callback. I removed extra forks: vets are no longer tied to services, and the specialist step and the clinic switcher are gone — after choosing a service the visitor goes straight to the form. People get in touch wherever it’s convenient: by phone, in Telegram or another messenger. Online consultations have their own flow with a choice of preferred time.

  1. Result

Metrics are being collected. The landing page has launched as an alternative to the main website, and conversion into requests is now being compared. The first results will appear here as soon as they are ready.
