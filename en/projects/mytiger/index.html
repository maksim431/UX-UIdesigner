my tiger

“My Tiger” is a pet beauty and health clinic in Moscow: veterinary care and grooming under one roof. I designed a separate landing page for the clinic to collect booking requests — an alternative to the main website that can be run in ads and compared with it on conversion.

in this case

Three hypotheses — and why the one without discounts went into production.

Grooming as a medical procedure — how to show that a haircut starts with caring for health, not with scissors.

Promotions without discounts — how to fit special offers into a premium brand without lowering its status.

Metrics are being collected — the landing page is live, results will appear here.

  1. Task

Design a landing page that converts visitors into booking requests for appointments and grooming — as an alternative to the clinic’s main website.

The client’s constraints: brand patterns (tiger silhouettes and claw scratches) and real photos, a premium style and unconventional design solutions. A separate goal was to show the clinic’s approach: grooming here is not a cosmetic service but a full medical procedure that requires a professional approach.

  1. Research

To understand what stops people from leaving a request, I looked at the audience, the market and the brand’s constraints.

The audience chooses by trust. Dog and cat owners for whom the pet is part of the family. Price is secondary: what matters is who will treat and groom their pet, in what conditions, and how animals are treated here. Takeaway: people will leave a request once they trust the clinic — so the landing page has to show its people, interiors and approach.

The market sells grooming as “wash and trim”. Salons compete on price, and the medical side of grooming is never shown. Takeaway: showing the link between grooming and veterinary care will set the clinic apart and justify its prices.

Discounts contradict premium. “−30%” and crossed-out prices are the usual way to attract customers, but in the premium segment they read as a sign of cheapness. Yet the clinic still needs special offers. Takeaway: we need a format of special offers that sells without a discount.

Everyone has their own entry point. One comes for a service, another for a vet they trust, a third for a special offer. Takeaway: every path has to lead to a short request.

  1. Hypotheses

From the research takeaways I formulated three versions of the landing page.

  1. “Discount funnel”. Like most clinics: a bright discount banner on the first screen, promotions and countdowns. If we offer a deal, there will be more requests.

  2. “Service catalog”. A detailed price list and the familiar booking flow: service → vet → clinic → time. If everything is transparent, people will decide faster.

  3. “Trust and short booking”. Show the clinic through its people, interiors and philosophy, put grooming next to veterinary care, present special offers as care — and make booking short from anywhere on the page. If owners trust the clinic and don’t get tired of the form, they will leave a request.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I chose the third hypothesis — “Trust and short booking”. I compared the options against the task and the research takeaways:

“Discount funnel” brings requests quickly but hits the most important thing — the premium status — and attracts bargain hunters rather than the clients the clinic was built for.

“Service catalog” is transparent but repeats the main website: comparing two identical approaches makes no sense. A long booking flow loses people along the way, and in a price list grooming again looks like “just another service”.

“Trust and short booking” answers all the takeaways at once: it builds trust, shows grooming as medicine, solves the promotions problem and shortens the path to a request. It also differs most from the main website, so the conversion comparison will be fair.

I took the best from the rejected options: from the first — special offers as a reason to book, but without a discount; from the second — “from” prices right in the service list when booking, to keep things transparent.

  1. Designing the mockups

Every decision in the mockups follows from the chosen hypothesis.

  1. A premium style with brand patterns (UI). To build trust at first glance: deep green, warm milky white and chocolate — a palette that echoes the clinic’s interiors. Tiger silhouettes and claw scratches live on the dark side panel and in the details: the logo, dividers and accents. Real photos of pets and specialists instead of stock images.

  1. An unconventional layout (UI/UX). On the left is a pinned dark panel with the pattern and the clinic’s “Compliment”, so the special offer is always at hand. On the right are the first screen with the logo and a booking button, the philosophy, online consultations, services, the interior, vets and groomers, reviews and clinics on a map. Booking opens on top of the page over a photo of an exam room — the visitor keeps the context and feels like they are already in the clinic.

  2. Grooming as a medical procedure (UX/content). Veterinary services and grooming stand side by side as equals — in the “Services” block and in a shared “Vets and groomers” team. “Our philosophy” says directly that the groomers are trained in the specifics of each breed and work together with the vets, so every haircut is also a check of the pet’s health.

  1. “Compliments” instead of promotions (UX/content). Special offers are called the clinic’s compliments: a special price and validity dates, with no percentages or crossed-out prices. Each compliment opens as an expert article: for example, the gastro check-up starts with the symptoms worth paying attention to, then shows what the program includes and the price. The owner gets care and expertise rather than a discount.

  2. Short booking from anywhere (UX). You can book through a service, through a specialist, via a compliment, or leave a number for a callback. People get in touch wherever it’s convenient: by phone, in Telegram or another messenger.

  1. Presenting and defending to the client

I presented the concept not as a set of screens but as a chain: research takeaway → hypothesis → decision in the mockup. That way every decision was justified by the task rather than by taste.

Defended the “Compliments”. The main question was how to sell without discounts. I showed it with the gastro check-up: the owner first learns about their pet’s symptoms and only then sees the program and the special price — the offer works and the brand’s status doesn’t suffer.

Made revisions after the discussion. We simplified booking even further: vets were untied from services, so the specialist step disappeared from booking via services — after choosing a service the visitor goes straight to the form. We removed the clinic switcher, and booking via a vet now only asks for the clinic. We added a new flow — an online consultation with a choice of preferred time.

  1. Handoff to development

So that developers wouldn’t have extra questions, I organized the file by scenario:

  • desktop (1920) and mobile (390) versions of the page and the menu;

  • each booking flow — via services, via vets, via compliments, and the online consultation — as a separate section with every step: choice, details, “request received”;

  • alternative states, for example when Telegram is chosen as the contact method;

  • all modals: philosophy, specialist profile, compliments, callback;

  • revisions after the presentation are marked right in the mockups, so it’s clear what changed and why.

  1. Results

Metrics are being collected. The landing page has launched as an alternative to the main website, and conversion into requests is now being compared. The first results will appear here as soon as they are ready — and will show whether the “Trust and short booking” hypothesis was confirmed.
