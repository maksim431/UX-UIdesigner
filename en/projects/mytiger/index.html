my tiger

“My Tiger” is a pet beauty and health clinic in Moscow: veterinary care and grooming under one roof. I designed a separate landing page for the clinic to collect booking requests — an alternative to the main website that can be run in ads and compared with it on conversion.

  1. Task

Design a landing page that converts visitors into booking requests for appointments and grooming — as an alternative to the clinic’s main website. The main website tells everything about the clinic; the landing page has one job — to bring in requests from ads.

The client’s constraints: brand patterns (tiger silhouettes and claw scratches) and real photos, a premium style and unconventional design solutions.

A separate goal: show the clinic’s approach — grooming here is not a cosmetic service but a full medical procedure that requires a professional approach.

  1. Research

To understand what stops people from leaving a request, I looked at the audience, the market and the entry points.

The audience chooses by trust. Dog and cat owners for whom the pet is part of the family. Price is secondary; what decides is “who will treat and groom my pet”, “in what conditions” and “how animals are treated here”. Takeaway: people leave a request once trust is built — so the landing page has to show the people, the place and the approach quickly.

Grooming is sold as a cosmetic service. Salons compete on price and promise to “wash and trim”. A premium owner cares more about the groomer noticing a skin, ear or coat problem and passing the pet to a vet. Takeaway: the “groomer + vet” combination is the clinic’s main differentiator and has to be shown as the reason to book here.

Discounts conflict with premium. “−30%” and crossed-out prices are the fastest way to get requests, but in the premium segment they read as a sign of cheapness and attract bargain hunters. Takeaway: special offers are needed, but without the language of discounts.

Entry points differ. One person comes for a service, another for a specific vet, a third for a special offer, a fourth isn’t ready to visit and wants a consultation first. Takeaway: each of these paths has to lead to a short request with no extra steps.

  1. Hypotheses

From the research takeaways I put together three landing page concepts.

Hypothesis A — a “promo landing page”. A bright discount offer on the first screen and a booking form. It brings requests quickly in any niche, but contradicts the premium positioning and attracts the wrong audience.

Hypothesis B — a “short version of the main website”. The same sections, just more compact. Safe for the brand, but the landing page would hardly differ from the website, and the test wouldn’t show what works better.

Hypothesis C — “trust and a short path”. First we build trust through the clinic’s people, interiors and approach — vets and groomers as one team. Booking is available from anywhere on the page in a few steps, and special offers are presented as care — the clinic’s “Compliments”.

  1. Which hypothesis I chose and why

I went with hypothesis C, because it’s the only one that answers all the research takeaways:

  • the audience chooses by trust — the concept builds trust before asking for a request;

  • the clinic’s main differentiator — grooming overseen by vets — becomes a reason to book rather than a line in the price list;

  • special offers stay, but don’t lower the brand’s status;

  • the landing page clearly differs from the main website, so the conversion comparison will be fair.

Hypothesis A was rejected because of the conflict with the brand, hypothesis B — because it gives nothing new to compare.

  1. Designing the mockups

I checked every decision in the mockups with one question: how does it bring the visitor closer to a request?

  1. First screen: who we are and what to do. A large logo, the positioning “Pet beauty and health clinic”, a real photo and a “Book an appointment” button.

    For conversion: within a couple of seconds it’s clear what this place is and what the main action is — the booking button is visible without scrolling.

  1. A pinned panel with the “Compliment”. On the left is a dark panel with the brand’s tiger pattern and the clinic’s current compliment; it stays on screen while scrolling.

    For conversion: a reason to book is always in view, but without pop-ups and banners that annoy and cheapen. And the brand pattern meets the client’s requirement and makes the page recognizable.

  1. Booking on top of the page over an exam room. The form opens as a layer over the landing page: choose a service, a short form, confirmation. The background is a photo of the clinic’s exam room.

    For conversion: no jump to another page — fewer chances to lose the visitor on the way. The exam room photo keeps building trust right at the moment of decision.

  1. A short form and a convenient contact channel. Minimal fields: name, phone, pet details. People choose where to be contacted — by phone, in Telegram or another messenger. For those with no time for the form, there’s a callback.

    For conversion: every extra field lowers the share of people who complete the form, and a familiar channel raises the chance that they’ll answer the administrator after leaving a request.

  1. Grooming next to veterinary care. Veterinary services and grooming stand side by side as equals, and vets and groomers are shown as one team: experience, specialization, a detailed profile. “Our philosophy” says it plainly: groomers are trained in the specifics of each breed and work together with vets, so every haircut is also a health check.

    For conversion: it removes the main fear — trusting a pet to strangers — and turns grooming from a “haircut for a price” into a service a premium client is willing to pay for.

  1. “Compliments” instead of discounts. Special offers are the clinic’s compliments: a special price and validity dates, with no percentages or crossed-out prices. Each one opens as an expert article. For example, the gastro check-up: first the symptoms worth paying attention to, then what the program includes and its price.

    For conversion: the validity dates give a reason to book now, and the expert text explains why the pet needs it. People buy care, not a discount — and the brand keeps its status.

  1. Online consultation. A separate block with its own booking flow: choose a specialist, enter details and a preferred time.

    For conversion: an extra entry point for those not yet ready to visit the clinic — a small first step that makes an in-person visit easier later.

  1. Reviews and clinics on a map. Reviews with the Yandex Maps rating, clinic addresses, opening hours and a “Get directions” button — at the end of the page.

    For conversion: social proof and the answer to “how far is it” appear exactly where the visitor makes the decision.

  1. Presentation and defense

I walked the client through the concept on a live prototype — a working page rather than static images — along the same chain: what the research revealed, what the hypotheses were, why hypothesis C was chosen and how each decision works toward a request.

Dropping classic discounts. The least obvious decision for a clinic that needs special offers. The argument: a discount attracts bargain hunters, while a “Compliment” with an expert explanation and validity dates gives the same reason to book and keeps the positioning.

Simplifying booking after the discussion. We removed everything from the flow that doesn’t help reach a request: vets are no longer tied to services, and the specialist step and the clinic switcher are gone — after choosing a service the visitor goes straight to the form. We also added a separate online consultation flow.

  1. Handoff to development

I handed off mockups for desktop and phone, all modals (booking via services and via specialists, compliments, philosophy, specialist profile, callback) and every step of the booking flows. The modals are built as components, and the changes after the presentation are annotated right in the mockups, so the developer can see what changed and why.

  1. Results

Metrics are being collected. The landing page has launched as an alternative to the main website. We’re comparing conversion into requests between the landing page and the website, and looking at which entry points bring more requests: services, specialists, compliments or online consultation. The first results will appear here as soon as they are ready.

  1. Where and how I used AI

  • Data analysis. I ran reviews of vet clinics and grooming salons through AI and clustered owners’ fears: who to trust with the pet, the animal’s stress, “they groom but don’t look at health”. These clusters fed the research takeaways.

  • A live coded prototype. With AI I turned the mockups into a working landing page prototype — with the booking flows, “Compliments” and modals. I used it to defend the decisions to the client and to run user tests before development.

  • Structure of the “Compliments”. AI helped lay out the expert articles in a clear scheme — from symptoms to program and price; facts and wording came from the clinic.

  • Figma routine. An AI agent handled layer naming, turning modals into components and checking states across all booking steps.
