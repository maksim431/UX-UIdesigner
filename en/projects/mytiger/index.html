my tiger

“My Tiger” is a beauty and health clinic for animals in Moscow, with veterinary care and grooming under one roof. I designed a separate landing page for the clinic to collect booking requests. It can run in ads and be compared with the main website by conversion.

  1. Project at a glance

  • Role. I was the only designer on the team and was responsible for all landing page design.

  • Timeline. 5 days from the briefing to handoff to development and the next iteration.

  • Starting point. A new landing page that runs alongside the clinic’s main website. It runs in ads and is compared with the website by conversion.

  • What I did. Research, the concept, desktop and mobile mockups, all booking flows, a live coded prototype and handoff to development.

  • Outcome. A landing page with four entry points to booking, component-based modals and a prototype on which I defended the decisions before development.

  1. Task and constraints

The task was to design a landing page that brings booking requests for vet visits and grooming from ads. The main website tells everything about the clinic. The landing page had to give the same information but bring more requests.

Client requests: brand patterns (tiger silhouettes and claw scratches), real photos, a premium style and unusual solutions. I also had to show that grooming here is not cosmetic but a full medical procedure.

Technical limitation: all functionality and all content must be placed in the landing page, without additional pages.

  1. What I found out

People choose a clinic by trust. For owners a pet is a family member. Price matters less to them; they want to know who will treat and groom the pet, in what conditions and how animals are treated here. So the landing page has to show the people, the place and the approach quickly.

Grooming is sold as cosmetics. Salons compete on price. But a premium owner cares more that the groomer notices a problem and passes the pet to a vet. I decided that the “groomer + vet” pairing is the clinic’s main difference.

Discounts hurt the premium image. “−30%” and crossed-out prices bring people looking for something cheaper. So special offers are needed, but without the language of discounts.

People come with different goals. One looks for a service, another for a vet, a third came for a special offer, a fourth wants a consultation first. So there has to be a short path to a request from any of these places.

  1. Three hypotheses and how I chose

To compare the hypotheses I scored them with ICE. Impact: how strongly the hypothesis would move the key metrics, which are the number of requests and trust in the clinic. Scored from 1 to 10. Confidence: how sure I am of the effect given the research data, from 0 to 100%. Ease: how much time, resources and team effort it takes, from 1 to 10, where 10 is easiest. The total score is Impact × Confidence × Ease.

Hypothesis A. A promo landing page. A discount on the first screen and a form. This kind of page brings requests quickly in any niche, but it goes against the clinic’s positioning and attracts the wrong audience.
Impact 4/10 · Confidence 40% · Ease 9/10 → ICE 14.4

Hypothesis B. A short version of the website. It is safe for the brand, but the landing page would barely differ from the website, and the test wouldn’t show what works better.
Impact 3/10 · Confidence 60% · Ease 8/10 → ICE 14.4

Hypothesis C. Trust and a short path. First show the people, the interiors and the clinic’s approach, where vets and groomers work as one team. You can book from anywhere on the page in a few steps. Special offers are presented as care, as the clinic’s “Compliments”.
Impact 8/10 · Confidence 70% · Ease 5/10 → ICE 28

I chose C because it has the highest ICE score: 28 versus 14.4 and 14.4. It is the only one that answers all four research findings and allows a fair comparison of the landing page with the website. Its riskiest part is dropping regular discounts, and I discussed that with the client separately at the review.

  1. Where I was wrong and what I changed

Booking was longer than it needed to be. In the first version the booking flow copied the main website’s logic: service, specialist, clinic, form. At the review the client and I went through this flow on the live prototype. It became clear that choosing a specialist and the clinic switch only made the path longer and didn’t help people get to a request.

I redid the flow. I unlinked vets from services and removed the specialist step and the clinic switch. Now after choosing a service the person goes straight to the form. I moved the online consultation into a separate short flow. I noted all changes right in the mockup so the developer could see what changed and why.

  1. Decisions and what they target

  1. First screen: who we are and what to do. A large logo, the positioning “Beauty and health clinic for animals”, a live video and a “Book an appointment” button.

    What it targets: within a couple of seconds it is clear what this place is and what the main action is.

  1. A pinned panel with a “Compliment”. On the left there is a dark panel with the brand tiger pattern and the clinic’s current compliment. It stays on screen while the person scrolls.

    What it targets: the reason to book is always visible. And there are no pop-ups that annoy people and make the brand look cheaper.

  1. Booking on top of the page. The form opens as a layer over the landing page: service, short form, confirmation. In the background there is a photo of a clinic exam room.

    What it targets: the person doesn’t leave the page, so there is less chance of losing them. And the exam room photo adds trust at the moment they decide.

  1. A short form and a convenient contact channel. Name, phone, pet details and a choice of how to be contacted: phone, Telegram or another messenger. People in a hurry can request a callback. You can’t pick an exact time in the form, so I didn’t make it look like online booking. The form just collects contact details, and the administrator agrees on the time in the channel the person prefers.

    What it targets: more people filling in the form and replying to the administrator after the request.

  1. Grooming next to veterinary care. Veterinary services and grooming are shown as equals, and vets and groomers are presented as one team with experience and specializations. The “Our philosophy” section says directly that every grooming session is also a health check.

    What it targets: remove the main fear of trusting a pet to strangers, and make grooming a service a premium client is willing to pay for.

  1. “Compliments” instead of discounts. A special price and an end date, but no percentages or crossed-out prices. Each compliment opens as an expert article: symptoms, program and price.

    What it targets: the end date gives a reason to book now, and the brand keeps its status.

  1. Online consultation. A separate block with its own short flow: a specialist vet, details and a convenient time.

    What it targets: one more entry point for people who aren’t ready to come to the clinic yet.

  1. Reviews and clinics on the map. At the end of the page there are reviews with a rating on Yandex Maps, addresses, opening hours and a “Get directions” button.

    What it targets: other owners’ reviews and the answer to “how far is it” are right where the person makes the decision.

  1. Defending the design and handoff

I showed the client the concept on a live prototype, that is, on a working page and not on pictures. I explained it in order: findings, hypotheses, the choice and how each decision helps get a request.

Dropping regular discounts was the least obvious decision for a clinic that needs special offers. I explained it like this: a discount attracts people looking for something cheaper, while a “Compliment” with an expert explanation and an end date also gives a reason to book but doesn’t hurt the positioning.

I handed over desktop and mobile mockups, all modals and every step of the booking flows. The modals are built from components, and changes after the review are noted in the mockup.

How I work. In five days I took the project from the briefing to handoff to development on my own. I defend debatable decisions on a working prototype, because that makes them easier to discuss.

  1. Results

The landing page is live alongside the main website, and metrics are being collected. We compare request conversion with the website and look at which entry points bring more requests. When the numbers come in, I will add them here.

Product: how the product works and how people use it

  • Four entry points to booking: services, specialists, “Compliments” and online consultation. Targets growth in the total number of requests.

  • Booking without leaving the page and with a short flow after it was simplified at the review. Targets more people getting from a click to a request.

  • Choice of contact channel and a callback. Targets people replying to the administrator after the request.

  • A reason to book is always visible: the pinned panel with a “Compliment”. Targets request conversion on a long page.

Communication: how the product explains its value to users

  • Trust before the request: the people, interiors and the clinic’s approach are visible from the first screens.

  • Grooming as a medical procedure: the “groomer + vet” pairing works as a reason to book.

  • Special offers without the language of discounts: “Compliments” explain why it matters for the pet and keep the premium status.

  • A recognizable brand: the tiger pattern and real photos meet the client’s request and make the page stand out in ads.

  1. How I used AI

  • Claude: data collection and analysis. Reviews of vet clinics and grooming salons. From them I picked out owners’ main fears: who to trust the pet to, stress for the animal, and salons that groom but don’t look at health. The main benefit is quality: before, I collected data from a small number of sources, while Claude covers many more, so the conclusions rest on a fuller picture. About 5 minutes instead of a full day of manual searching.

  • Claude: segmentation and internal metrics analysis. I broke down the metrics of the clinic’s main website: requests by service, traffic source and owner segment (dogs, cats, grooming, veterinary care). That showed me which entry points matter more on the landing page. About 2 minutes instead of 2 hours of work in Excel.

  • Claude: a live coded prototype. From the mockups I built a working page with the booking flows and modals. It is very close to the future product, and it can be used for testing. About 20 minutes instead of 6 hours on an interactive prototype in Figma, where complex animation often can’t be shown at all.

  • Claude + Figma Agent: preparing for development. Styles, layer naming, responsive versions, building the design system and components in all states, finding missing screens and states, checking the user path logic and contrast, comments for developers. About 1 hour including edits instead of 16 hours of manual work.

AI helps me with data collection and routine work. I make the decisions myself and check them myself.
